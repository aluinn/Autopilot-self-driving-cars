# Training and fitness

How a car's score is computed, and how the shipped demo brain was made. Everything here is read
from [`car.js`](../car.js), [`evolution.js`](../evolution.js) and [`tools/train-demo.js`](../tools/train-demo.js).

There are no external datasets: the tracks are hand-authored centre lines in
[`tracks.js`](../tracks.js) and the demo brain is generated by the script. (That is why this repo has no `DATA_SOURCES.md`.)

## Fitness

```
fitness = checkpoints passed
        + progress toward the next checkpoint   (0 to 0.99)
        + 0.5 * (average speed / max speed)
```

- *Checkpoints passed* is the main signal and counts across laps, so a second lap is worth more than a first.
- *Progress* is `1 - distance to the next checkpoint / gap between the two surrounding checkpoints`, clamped.
  It breaks ties between cars on the same checkpoint.
- *Speed* is worth at most half a checkpoint, a nudge toward brisk driving that can't outweigh actually getting further.

Cars stop (permanently) on a wall hit, on crossing the previous checkpoint backwards, or after 180 ticks without a
new checkpoint. That last rule is what stops "park and do nothing" from being a stable strategy.

## Breeding

See the comment at the top of [`evolution.js`](../evolution.js): 5 elite copies, then children from the top quarter,
chosen with probability proportional to `fitness + 0.01`; half are crossovers (each weight from either parent), all are
mutated (each weight has a `rate` chance of being nudged by up to ±0.5).

## How the demo brain is trained

`npm run train-demo` runs 80 generations of 100 brains, using the same car, sensor, network and evolution code as the site
but with no drawing. Differences from interactive play, all aimed at a brain that generalises:

1. **Every brain is tested on all three presets**, with scores converted to "laps' worth" so the short and long tracks count equally.
2. **The score is the total plus three times the worst track**, so a brain can't win by acing two tracks and ignoring the hardest one.
3. **The final pick is the most robust of the last 10 generations' top 5**: the one whose *worst* result over 3 tracks × 7 slightly
   different starting positions and angles is best. This guards against brains that only work from the exact start pose.

The script rewrites `demo.js`. I did not re-run it while writing these docs, so the current `demo.js` is exactly what was
already in the repo. The unit test `the demo brain passes checkpoints on every preset` checks that it still gets over half a lap on each preset
in 1200 ticks, and does better than a random brain.

## What this does not tell you

- No statistics on how often training succeeds from scratch. From two headless runs on Medium at 20×, the first lap appeared at generation 5 and at generation 22. That is anecdote, not a benchmark.
- Generalisation to arbitrary drawn tracks has not been measured.
